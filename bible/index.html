01

Website research and history

What the site documents

  • 1921, Kozhikode. Kaloor Neelakandan Vaidyar founded AVVVS as a pharmacy preparing and dispensing Ayurveda medicines. (About Us; Home)
  • Sree Narayana Guru. The site says the founder was "personally blessed and advised" by the Guru to set up a patient-care facility. This is AVVVS's own account. No image of the Guru appears on the site.
  • Life dates. Founder 1895–1967. Ramdas Vaidyar 1934–1998. Both come from the centenary banner graphic only.
  • Succession. His son K. N. Ramadas Vaidyar succeeded him and was known in Kozhikode's socio-literary circles for satire. Present management: Dr Manoj Kaloor (Managing Director) and Reeja Manoj.
  • Portfolio. "More than 300" classical and proprietary formulations. Named products: Mezhu Thailam, Rujanth (capsules and liniment), Slepan, Red Irons, Lakshol, Diastat, Kidcare, Koftie, Hair Greens.
  • Factory. GMP-certified, in the city suburbs. Lines named: capsule filling, blister packing, ointment manufacture and filling, cosmetics, plus traditional mechanised units. Exports to Russia, Austria, Slovenia and Colombia.
  • Cosmetics. The /cosmetic/ page already sells under Gloveda: face washes, scrub, cream, scar oil and anti-dandruff shampoo, plus AVVVS face pack, moisturiser and Hairgreens shampoo. Herbs named include Triphala, Tulasi, Vacha, Manjishta, Yashtimadhu, Haridra, Bhringaraja and Amla.
  • Milestones seen in photos. Navathi (90th year) celebrations, 2011, from event signage and a press clipping dated 26 September 2011. Centenary event branded "Ayursatham 22", Kozhikode.

Problems in the source material

The "unmatched legacy" article claims "tens of thousands of acres of herbal farms" and a "seed-to-shelf" business. The rest of the site only mentions herbal gardens around the factory, so this looks like copied boilerplate. The same article says "98 years". I've kept both claims out of the film.

Not on the site at all: Gloveda Experience, Gloveda Revive, third-party manufacturing and white-label manufacturing. Sequence 08 needs a short brief for each before I can design those destinations.

The real archive is thin. There are two portraits, each about 250 px wide. There are no photographs from 1921, no image of the original premises, and no manuscripts. The earliest building image is a 2011 newspaper clipping of a shopfront.

The AVVVS logo is blue with a green leaf mark. The film palette is lamp-black, rosewood and gold. Sequence 07 needs a logo treatment that brand owners approve (decision 08).

Pages read: home, about-us, gallery and all 6 sub-galleries, cosmetic, speciality clinic, treatments, and 8 articles. Raw HTML, 119 downloaded assets and contact sheets are in research/.

04

Production pipeline recommendation

Since the website photos are off the table, real footage is the film's credibility. A one-day live shoot in Kozhikode does more for quality than any render engine. It covers the hand, the real leaf and the real packs. Everything that claims to be real is filmed. Everything metaphorical is built, including manufacturing, which is shown as precision rather than as a place.

  • Live action: the hand, the leaf on a lightbox, the oil-drop high-speed shot, products. Shots 01.05, 02.01, 05.02–05.04.
  • Unreal Engine 5: the forest only (01.02–01.04). It's the one place where dense vegetation, volumetric light and a long continuous camera move justify UE5. Kerala-specific species need custom scans.
  • Blender: the inner, symbolic world. Venation, palm-leaf incisions, golden threads, the droplet, the product plinth extension, the precision sequence (06), the network reveal.
  • Post typography: the heritage sequence. Names, dates and the Guru's blessing, set with restraint.
  • Three.js / R3F: Sequences 07–08 on the website, matched to the film's final frame.
  • AI video: fallback only, for forest plates if UE5 slips. Never for people, heritage, products or the factory.

05

Treatment

A leaf is taken from the forest. Its veins become a line of script. The script becomes a family's century. The century becomes one drop of medicine, and that drop becomes the exact, repeatable craft of a modern manufacturer that still makes it.

The film is one continuous camera with no hard cuts. Every transition is physical: a vein becomes an incision, an incision becomes a thread, a thread becomes a drop, a ripple becomes a machined ring. In Sequence 06 the edit rhythm tightens, so precision is felt in the timing. The factory itself is never shown. Its facts appear as type.

The film rejects the "golden fantasy temple". Gold is used in two places only: the threads and the droplet. Everywhere else, gold is just the colour of low sun. The forest is a working Western Ghats forest with buttress roots, lianas, wild ginger and moss. It is not enchanted.

Heritage is told in words, not invented pictures. Kozhikode, 1921, three names, two milestones. The type is set in lamp-black, the soot that Kerala scribes rubbed into palm leaves to make writing visible. That is the film's honest link between knowledge and lineage.

Changes I've made to the brief

Palm leaf instead of parchment. Kerala's medical knowledge was carried on palm-leaf manuscripts, incised with a stylus and blackened with soot. A vein turning into an incised line is a more accurate image than ink on parchment, and a more striking one.

Botanical plates are labelled as illustration. Palm-leaf texts rarely carry drawings, so Sequence 04's ingredient plates are new engravings in a Malabar botanical style. The Hortus Malabaricus (Amsterdam, 1678–1693), compiled with Kerala physicians, is the stylistic reference. It is never presented as an AVVVS document.

No faces in heritage unless you supply originals. With the website portraits excluded, the honest options are type only, or illustrated portraits clearly captioned as illustrations (decision 03).

The hand is real. AI hands fail at macro scale and would undercut the "sacred and intimate" moment. I recommend the hand of a practising AVVVS physician.

Light

Low sun, 6–10° elevation, through gaps. Shadows fall to warm lamp-black, never to grey or green. One key source per shot. Sequence 06 moves to a cooler key (5600K) against a warm residual rim (3200K), so tradition and technology show up in colour temperature.

Camera language

Slow and continuous: cranes, push-ins and long lateral dollies. Primes from 24 to 100 mm macro. No handheld. Sequence 06 uses locked-off, symmetrical frames on a tighter rhythm.

Film touchstones

Microcosmos (1996) for macro scale and patience. Samsara (2011) for formal, reverent framing. The Tree of Life (2011) for light through leaves. These are for tone, not imitation.

Typography

A restrained serif for names and dates, in ivory or lamp-black. Malayalam and English set in parallel where the name appears. All type is added in post. None is generated in footage.

Grade

No green in the grade. Blacks sit warm toward rosewood and highlights stay gold and ivory. Foliage can't stop being green, so it is pulled down to a deep, desaturated olive-umber that reads as shadow, not colour. Overall saturation is down about 15%, except on gold. 35mm-style grain under 3% so it is felt, not seen.

Banned

Lens flares as decoration, floating particles everywhere, temple imagery, glowing runes, invented script, generic "doctor in a white coat", stock smiling families.

AVVVS reference stills

From avvvs.com. Reference only. Not used in the film. They guide the live shoot and packaging accuracy.

09

Blender, Three.js and UE5 requirements

Unreal Engine 5.8 · forest

  • Shots 01.02–01.04. One level with a 40 × 25 m playable area. The camera never sees past 60 m, so the rest is fog.
  • Nanite foliage. PCG scatter for buttress trees, lianas, ferns, wild ginger and moss.
  • Hero plant and leaf litter photogrammetry captured on the shoot day, so the species is real and Kerala-specific. Library foliage alone won't be.
  • Lumen with hardware ray tracing. Local volumetric fog for light shafts. Niagara dust used sparingly, only inside light shafts.
  • Sequencer with CineCamera: 24 mm and 35 mm full-frame equivalents, f/5.6–8, 24 fps, 180° shutter.
  • Movie Render Queue to EXR in ACEScg. 1080p locally for previs and review. 4K finals on a 24 GB+ cloud GPU, because the RTX 4050's 6 GB will limit foliage density.

Blender 5.2 · symbolic world

  • Venation (02.02): space-colonisation curves in Geometry Nodes, seeded from the scanned real leaf. Emissive sap flow along the curves.
  • Incision morph (02.03): curves projected onto a scanned palm-leaf mesh, with displacement and an animated soot mask.
  • Threads (04.01–04.04): vectorised plate linework becomes curves, then a braid solver drawn as thin tubes with a gold thin-film shader. Count stays under 200.
  • Droplet (04.04–05.01): SDF and metaball surface, wobble from a simple spring sim. Transmission plus amber volume absorption, refracting the plates.
  • Cycles with OptiX on the RTX 4050. Adaptive sampling with denoise. Output EXR multilayer, ACEScg.
  • I can drive Blender headless from this machine, so lookdev iterations don't need a manual step.

Three.js / R3F · website

  • Sequence 07 continues the film's last frame. The film ends on a plate the web scene matches in camera, colour and thread positions.
  • Threads as instanced tube geometry with a custom shader (curl-noise drift, fresnel gold). Under 60k triangles.
  • Four destinations, each a distinct world (see Seq 08). Loaded on demand, under 2.5 MB each, KTX2 textures, Draco meshes.
  • GSAP ScrollTrigger to scrub the film segments. Video as HLS at 1080p, with 720p for mobile.
  • Static poster frames and reduced motion for low-power devices and prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Skills to apply: awwwards-motion (shaders, particles, post), scroll-experience, and one of impeccable or hallmark for the page design.

Recommendation

Commission a composer who works with Kerala percussion, and record the uruli tone on the shoot. An AI temp track is fine for the animatic. It shouldn't be the final score.

Temp music prompt

Copy temp music prompt
Cinematic instrumental, 2 minutes 49 seconds, 60 to 72 BPM. Opens with near silence and a soft forest ambience, then a low cello and harmonium-like drone. A restrained, melancholy melody on solo viola over a pitched Indian hourglass drum (edakka style). Gradually divided strings rise with a quiet, accelerating frame-drum pulse. At 1:41 everything converges on one sustained bowed bronze bell tone, followed by a full beat of silence. Then a precise, mechanical staccato string section with percussive metallic textures. Ends with the opening melody in full strings, warm and dignified, resolving on a sustained chord. No vocals, no EDM drops, no trailer braams, no stock corporate piano.

Sound effects to record or license

  • Malabar whistling thrush, dawn chorus, cicadas, leaf drip (field recording, Kerala)
  • Leaf stem snap and fingertip on leaf (foley, macro mics)
  • Stylus incising palm leaf. Soot rubbed in with a cloth.
  • Thread tension "sings": bowed wire and glass rim, pitched to the score
  • Droplet: slowed oil-drop impact recorded at 96 kHz
  • Bronze uruli bowed and struck (signature tone)
  • Precision: struck and bowed steel, clockwork, glass tap, a calibrated click
  • Room tone of a quiet wood-floored room
